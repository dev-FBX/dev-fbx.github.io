Architecture

Where a question goes.

  1. 01

    It arrives through the portal

    The widget is the native Power Pages component; the conversation runs over Direct Line using the portal's own token.

  2. 02

    It searches only the base

    Open questions go to three Markdown files, one per language. Nothing comes from the internet.

  3. 03

    No citation, no answer

    The model writes from what it found and has to cite it. Without a citation, the fallback takes over and offers the contact form.

The hard call

Why it doesn't make things up, and where that stops.

The question everyone asks about agents. The honest answer has two sides.

What the configuration guarantees

  • The model's own knowledge and web search are switched off: an answer can only come from the three files written for it.
  • An answer that doesn't cite its source is withheld, and the fallback takes over, stating what's out of scope.
  • Content moderation set to high, which is also the platform default.

What it doesn't guarantee

  • It doesn't stop the model from blending general knowledge into the retrieved passage. The block only catches the turn where it consulted no source at all.
  • The block checks for a citation, not for truth: a correct answer without a citation gets discarded, and the same question asked again may go through.
  • Grounding reduces made-up answers; it doesn't eliminate them. That's why the scope is narrow: profile, projects and contact. Pricing and timelines are a conversation between people.
RAG in one sentence, and the recipe against discarded answers

Instead of trusting the model's memory, the agent retrieves the relevant passage from the base, hands it over with the question and requires the answer to come from it. The platform calls this a grounded generative answer. Microsoft's documented recipe for discarding fewer good answers: ask for the citation in the instructions and avoid a rigid output format, which suppresses the citation marker.

How it works inside
  • RAG: an open question triggers a search in the base; the passage found goes to the model together with the question, and the answer can only come from that passage.
  • Closed scope: profile, projects and contact. Anything outside that is routed to the contact form.
  • Fallback topic: the fallback takes over when the question is out of scope or when the answer carries no citation.
  • Mandatory citation: an answer without a source citation is withheld before it reaches the visitor.
  • Classic engine (standard harness): this is where this agent runs. It follows the topics, rules and paths defined for it, behaving predictably for known questions. It was chosen because it supports external publishing on Power Pages.
  • New experience (GitHub Copilot harness): it takes a goal, breaks it into steps, calls tools, connectors and other agents, and recovers when a step fails; it creates and edits Word, Excel, PowerPoint and PDF files. It requires the new Copilot Studio experience and has its own billing model: the rates under Licensing and cost apply only to the standard harness.
  • How to choose: a multi-step process, with files and decisions along the way, calls for the new experience; known questions, where the reply must come out the same every time, call for the classic one.
Governance: what comes built in, without writing a line
  • Access by group membership: the portal's web roles decide who sees the widget.
  • Authentication inherited from the portal, with no new password or separate sign-up.
  • No Microsoft 365 crawling: the agent can't reach anyone's email, files or calendar.
  • No analysis of files uploaded by the visitor.
  • The model isn't swapped on its own: upgrading is a decision, not a Monday-morning surprise.
  • Conversations and unanswered questions are logged in Copilot Studio analytics.
Licensing and cost
  • People who chat: nothing. Visitors don't need a license.
  • People who build: a Copilot Studio author license, or the maker role in the admin center.
  • To publish on the site: Copilot Credits in the tenant, prepaid pack or pay-as-you-go.
  • Rates: hand-written answer = 1 credit · model-generated = 2 · agent action = 5.
  • Price: a 25,000-credit pack for US$200/month, or US$0.01 per credit pay-as-you-go (checked on Oct 2, 2026; verify before quoting it in a proposal).
From prototype to production, in five stages
  • Discovery: the twenty most frequent questions, who answers them today and how much time that takes.
  • Knowledge base: gather, clean up and write the material. This is where the project is won or lost.
  • Closed pilot: one team using it for real, with answers reviewed by people who know the subject.
  • Security and scope: who gets access, what it won't answer and where it escalates when it gets stuck.
  • Go-live and measurement: track unanswered questions and feed them back into the base. An agent isn't delivered once; it's maintained.
Full technical sheet
  • Sign-in: Power Pages' own; anyone already signed in to the site doesn't sign in again.
  • Experience: standard harness, chosen because it supports external publishing on Power Pages.
  • Starting point: Copilot Studio's own agent template for Power Pages.
  • Topics: fifteen, two of them hand-written (the greeting and the portal user sign-out).
  • Integration: native Power Pages widget, with translated labels and light/dark theme matching the site.
