In 30 seconds

Personal content every day, with no operator.

Problem

Delivering personalized content at scale, billing, re-engaging people who drift away and answering questions, all without anyone on call.

Solution

Orchestration in n8n: Meta's message comes in through a webhook, the agent replies with context from Postgres, and ASAAS closes the billing loop.

Impact
~hundreds/day

Messages per day with no human operator. Delivery, billing and re-engagement run on their own; human time goes to content and product.

Order of magnitude of the production operation, not an exact count.

Architecture

From WhatsApp to billing, through a single orchestrator.

  1. 01

    It comes in on WhatsApp

    Meta delivers the message by webhook straight to n8n. No dashboard needs to be open.

  2. 02

    It replies with context

    The agent reads the history and memory in Postgres before writing. That's why it remembers the name and the request.

  3. 03

    It bills and closes the loop

    ASAAS issues the charge, and the payment webhook updates the subscription on its own.

The hard call

Running without an operator: what runs on its own, and what takes people.

Zero-touch doesn't mean zero people. It means choosing where a person steps in.

What runs on its own

  • Daily delivery: the reflection, with an image, goes out at the time each subscriber chose.
  • Conversation at any hour: the agent replies with the memory of who's on the other side (name, family, prayer requests).
  • Billing and re-engagement: ASAAS charges, the payment webhook updates the subscription, and anyone who drifts away gets an invitation to come back.

What still takes people

  • Content and product: that's where human time goes. The machine delivers; deciding what's worth delivering is a human job.
  • Responding to alerts: failures become logs, retries and a Telegram alert. Fixing the root cause is still someone's job.
  • What would change next time: queues, retries and observability from the first sprint. Here they came later, under pressure.
Why n8n and not Power Automate here
  • Its own product, not a company tenant: there's no Microsoft 365 behind it to reuse licenses and login.
  • Everything comes in through webhooks: Meta and ASAAS call the orchestration from outside. In Power Automate, the HTTP trigger is a premium connector, billed per user or per flow.
  • Self-hosted: n8n runs in Docker on its own server. Cost doesn't grow per execution, and the data stays on the server chosen for the product.
  • Code when needed: JavaScript code nodes handle what the ready-made blocks don't cover.
  • In a company that already lives in Microsoft 365, the math changes: Power Automate usually wins on governance and inherited login. The context decides, not the tool.

AI under the hood
  • Agent: n8n's AI Agent node with OpenAI models: gpt-4o-mini day to day, and gpt-4o when the model router decides the conversation needs more.
  • Short-term memory: conversation history in Postgres, a window of the last 10 exchanges per session.
  • Long-term memory (personal-memory RAG): subscriber facts extracted by an LLM and stored with an embedding (OpenAI text-embedding-3-small, 1,536 dimensions) in a pgvector column.
  • Semantic search: the incoming message is embedded too; a Postgres function retrieves facts by cosine similarity, with a 0.70 threshold and an ivfflat index, and merges them with the most recent facts.
  • Injected context: the agent answers with that context assembled. That's why it remembers the name, the prayer request and what the person shared weeks earlier.
  • What it isn't: it's not document RAG over PDFs or external knowledge bases; here RAG serves as the subscriber's personal memory.
Stack by layer
  • Channel: WhatsApp, through the Meta Cloud API.
  • Orchestration: self-hosted n8n in Docker, on Oracle Cloud.
  • Data: Postgres, with each subscriber's history and memory.
  • AI: agent with OpenAI models and memory.
  • Billing: ASAAS (a Brazilian payment platform), with payment webhooks.
  • Operations: logs, retries and Telegram alerts.
The product, from the subscriber's side
  • Language: the product serves its subscribers in Portuguese, for the Brazilian market.
  • Sign-up: the person shares their tradition (Catholic or Evangelical), preferred time and what they're going through.
  • Every day: a Bible reflection adapted to that moment in life, with an image and a verse.
  • On demand: a prayer recorded as audio, and conversation about prayer requests or whatever is on their mind.
  • Bible texts: established Portuguese translations (ONBV, NVI, ACF), matching the chosen tradition.
  • Opting out: replying “PARAR” (stop) mutes messages; “cancelar assinatura” (cancel subscription) ends billing.
  • Data: handled under Brazil's LGPD data protection law, according to the product's own site.
